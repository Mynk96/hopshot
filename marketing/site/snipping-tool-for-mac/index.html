Snipping Tool for Mac: every shortcut, and the best app

By Mayank Harsani, maker of Hopshot · Updated 17 November 2026

There's no app called Snipping Tool on a Mac, but the feature is built in. Press ⌘ Shift 4 and drag to snip part of the screen, or add Control to copy the snip straight to the clipboard, like Win+Shift+S on Windows.

If you've just moved from Windows, the table below maps every Snipping Tool habit to its Mac shortcut. After that: step-by-step guides, where the built-in tool falls short, and the snipping apps worth installing.

How to use the snipping tool on Mac

Snip part of the screen

  1. Press ⌘ Shift 4. The pointer turns into a crosshair.
  2. Drag over the area you want. Hold Space while dragging to move the box, or press Esc to cancel.
  3. Let go. A thumbnail appears in the bottom-right corner for a few seconds, then the file is saved to your Desktop.

Snip and copy-paste (no file)

  1. Press ⌘ Control Shift 4 and drag over the area.
  2. Click where you want the image: an email, Slack, a doc, a chat with ChatGPT.
  3. Press ⌘ V. Nothing is saved to your desktop.

This is the closest match to Win+Shift+S. The catch: the clipboard holds one image, and it's gone the moment you copy something else.

Snip one window

  1. Press ⌘ Shift 4, then press Space.
  2. Move the camera pointer over a window so it's highlighted, then click.
  3. Hold Option while clicking to leave out the window's shadow.

Use the Screenshot toolbar

Press ⌘ Shift 5 for a toolbar with buttons for the whole screen, a window or a selected area, plus screen recording. Options sets a timer, where snips are saved, and whether the floating thumbnail shows. You can also open it from Applications → Utilities → Screenshot. (Older guides call it Grab; Apple replaced Grab with Screenshot in macOS Mojave.)

Snip with the mouse only

Open Launchpad, search for Screenshot and click it. The same toolbar appears, so you can pick an area and click Capture without touching the keyboard.

Make the Mac snipping tool work more like Windows

Send every snip to the clipboard

  1. Press ⌘ Shift 5 and click Options.
  2. Under Save to, choose Clipboard.

Now ⌘ Shift 4 behaves like Win+Shift+S: snip, then paste. Switch it back the same way.

Save snips to a folder instead of the Desktop

  1. Create a folder, for example Pictures → Screenshots.
  2. Press ⌘ Shift 5 → Options → Other Location… and pick the folder.

Prefer Terminal? This does the same:

defaults write com.apple.screencapture location ~/Pictures/Screenshots
killall SystemUIServer

Draw on a snip

Click the thumbnail right after snipping to open Markup, which has pens, shapes, text and a crop tool. For a snip that's already saved, open it in Preview and click the Markup button (the pen tip icon) in the toolbar.

Copy text out of a snip

Open the snip in Preview, hover over the text and drag to select it, then press ⌘ C. macOS recognises text in images (Live Text) on macOS Monterey and later.

Mac snipping shortcut not working?

  • Check the shortcut is on: System Settings → Keyboard → Keyboard Shortcuts → Screenshots, and make sure the boxes are ticked.
  • Another app took the shortcut: screenshot apps and some utilities claim ⌘ Shift 4. Quit them and try again.
  • The thumbnail won't drag: see our fix for the floating thumbnail.
  • A third-party app captures only your wallpaper: give it permission in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Screen & System Audio Recording.
  • Still stuck: restart the Mac. It clears a stuck screenshot service more often than you'd expect.

Where the Mac snipping tool falls short

  • Your desktop fills up. Every snip lands there as a separate PNG, and after a week you have dozens.
  • The thumbnail is fragile. It disappears after a few seconds. If you drag it into an app and the drop fails, the snip is gone; we explain why in this guide.
  • The clipboard holds one snip. Snipping three things for one message means snip, paste, snip, paste.
  • Nothing reaches your phone. Getting a snip to your phone means AirDrop (iPhone only) or emailing yourself.
  • It's Mac-only. If you also use a Windows PC, you're back to different shortcuts there.

FAQ

Is there a Snipping Tool for Mac?

Not an app with that name, but every Mac has the same feature built in. Press ⌘ Shift 4 and drag to snip an area, or ⌘ Shift 5 to open the Screenshot toolbar, which is the closest thing to the Snipping Tool window.

What is the Snipping Tool shortcut on Mac?

⌘ Shift 4 snips an area and saves it as a file. Add Control (⌘ Control Shift 4) to copy the snip to the clipboard instead, which is what Win+Shift+S does on Windows.

How do I snip and copy-paste on a Mac?

Press ⌘ Control Shift 4, drag over the area, then press ⌘ V wherever you want it. Nothing is saved to your desktop.

Can I download the Windows Snipping Tool for Mac?

No. Microsoft's Snipping Tool only runs on Windows. Use the Mac's built-in shortcuts, or a snipping app made for Mac.

Is there a free snipping tool for Mac?

Yes. The built-in Screenshot tool is free on every Mac. Shottr is a popular free app if you want more editing.

Does a MacBook have a snipping tool?

Yes. MacBook Air, MacBook Pro, iMac and Mac mini all have the same built-in shortcuts: ⌘ Shift 3, 4 and 5.

How do I snip a single window on Mac?

Press ⌘ Shift 4, then press Space. The pointer turns into a camera; click the window you want.

Where do snips go on a Mac?

To your Desktop, named like "Screenshot 2026-11-17 at 10.15.03". Change it in ⌘ Shift 5 → Options → Save to.

How do I stop screenshots cluttering my Mac desktop?

Snip to the clipboard with Control added to the shortcut, change the save location to a folder, or use an app such as Hopshot that keeps snips on a shelf and deletes them on its own.

Can I use the same snipping tool on Mac and Windows?

Yes. Hopshot and Snagit both run on Mac and Windows. Hopshot is $10 once and covers up to three computers in any mix.
